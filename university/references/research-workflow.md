# Research Workflow

Use this reference when researching universities, admissions, and academic programs. The goal is to produce structured, current, source-traceable information rather than generic school descriptions.

## 1. Define The Research Request

Identify:

- country or region
- university or list of universities
- degree level: undergraduate, master's, doctoral, exchange, foundation, language program, or other
- major, department, faculty, school, or research area
- applicant background when relevant: nationality, education system, current grade or degree, language scores, target intake year
- requested output: single-school report, multi-school comparison, program list, admissions summary, or source inventory

If the request is broad, make a reasonable default scope and state it before presenting results.

## 2. Freshness Rule

For time-sensitive fields, default to information from 2025 or later, including pages, PDFs, prospectuses, admissions notices, catalogs, fee pages, scholarship rules, visa pages, gaokao policy notices, and third-party summaries.

- Use source date filters when the search tool supports them.
- Include `2025`, the current year, target intake year, or phrases like `current admissions` in search terms when helpful.
- Do not rely on pre-2025 information for time-sensitive fields unless the user asks for historical context or no newer source exists and the result is explicitly labeled historical.
- Stable background such as school history, institutional positioning, campus overview, and long-term academic strengths may use facts from a current official school profile page even when the events described occurred before 2025.
- If an official live page lacks a visible date, it may be used only when it clearly describes the current or target intake cycle; record the source date as unknown.
- Treat deadlines, tuition, language requirements, application materials, and scholarship rules without a 2025-or-later basis as `待核验`.

## 3. Source Priority

Use this order:

1. Official university pages.
2. Official admissions office, graduate school, faculty, department, program, catalog, and application portal pages.
3. Government, examination authority, qualification framework, or recognized application-system pages.
4. Official downloadable PDFs, prospectuses, handbooks, and policy documents.
5. Credible third-party ranking, media, encyclopedia, or study-abroad pages as supplemental background only.

Prefer the most specific official source. A department program page is usually more authoritative for program requirements than a general university overview page.

## 4. Search And Extraction

For each target university or program, collect:

- official names in English and local language when available
- country, city, campus, and official website
- institution type and high-level profile
- academic structure: faculties, schools, colleges, departments, graduate schools
- major/program names, degree level, language of instruction, duration, and program link
- undergraduate admissions route, requirements, materials, language requirements, application timeline, fees, and scholarships
- postgraduate admissions route, academic requirements, language or standardized test requirements, research proposal or supervisor rules, materials, deadlines, fees, and scholarships
- source title, URL, source type, source date or update date when visible, applicable year or intake, applicable applicant group, retrieval date, and evidence level

For high-impact or time-sensitive fields, use `source-evidence-ledger.md` and record field-level evidence. High-impact fields include admissions requirements, deadlines, fees, scholarships, language scores, visa rules, gaokao volunteer rules, enrollment plans, program codes, credential recognition, and professional licensing.

Profile-library background rules:

- `A` entries can support concise non-time-sensitive background summaries.
- `B` entries are background leads only; if used, say the precise official profile page is still `待补充`.
- `C` entries require a source-recheck warning and should be verified through the school official profile, information disclosure, or education authority page before strong wording.
- No profile-library entry can support admissions, program availability, fees, deadlines, scholarships, visa, credential recognition, or policy conclusions.

## 5. Synthesis

Use concise synthesis rather than copying source text.

- For one school: start with a school profile, then undergraduate admissions, postgraduate admissions, major/program information, and source notes.
- For multiple schools: start with a comparison table, then short school notes and source notes.
- For one major across schools: compare program names, degree levels, language of instruction, duration, requirements, deadlines, and official links.
- For admissions-only requests: focus on eligibility, application route, materials, language requirements, timeline, fees, scholarships, and official pages.

## 6. Uncertainty Handling

Use `待核验` when:

- no official source was found
- the page is inaccessible or requires login
- details vary by department, program, applicant type, intake, or year
- official pages conflict
- only third-party or outdated sources are available
- only pre-2025 sources are available for a time-sensitive field

Do not infer missing deadlines, fees, scores, or materials from another program unless the source explicitly says the rule applies.

## 7. Final Quality Check

Before finalizing, check:

- every high-impact claim has a source or is marked `待核验`
- every high-impact claim has field-level evidence when the task is verification, comparison, strategy, gaokao volunteer filling, overseas application, visa, credential recognition, fees, or deadlines
- official sources are clearly distinguished from non-official sources
- dates and intake years are explicit where relevant
- sources are from 2025 or later by default, or older sources are labeled historical
- older historical facts are used only for stable background from current official pages
- comparison fields are consistent across schools
- missing information is visible rather than silently omitted
